# Product

## Problem

School is essential, but academic recall alone does not give every child enough practice with reasoning, explaining, experimenting, creating, and making decisions. Families and educators need complementary experiences that make these capabilities visible through safe, meaningful activity.

## Target users

The initial audience is African children approximately ages 4–11. The hackathon uses fictional learners to test the product direction. Parents, guardians, and educators are important future stakeholders, but they are not account types in this MVP.

## Vision

ThinkLab Kids should feel like a collection of learning worlds where children explore a situation, make a choice, explain their thinking, receive useful feedback, and adapt. It should complement school rather than compete with it or claim to replace teachers.

## Learning philosophy

Explore → Think → Attempt → Explain → Feedback → Adapt → Create → Master

This is a product aspiration, not a claim that the current prototype measures mastery reliably.

## Demo learners

- Amara, 4: early foundations through counting, pictures, voice, and matching.
- Tobi, 6: computational thinking through robot sequencing and coding logic.
- Zara, 7: maths, reasoning, and decision-making through a Nigerian market mission.
- David, 9: strategy and planning through chess-inspired reasoning.
- Favour, 11: open-ended real-world problem solving.

Zara and Tobi are functional in the hackathon slice. The rest demonstrate future directions.

## Learning-world concept

A learning world gives a capability a context. A market can make money and decision-making concrete; a robot route can make sequence and precision visible. Context should be familiar and respectful, with Nigerian/African settings used naturally rather than as decoration or stereotype.

## Long-term vision

Future work may include more worlds, richer explanations, voice and accessibility support, guardian-informed progress views, and carefully governed persistence. Those require product validation, safety design, consent, and operational readiness first.

## Assumptions versus validated facts

The learning philosophy, audience, and fictional learner concepts are current product assumptions for the hackathon. They are not evidence of learning outcomes. Any claim that a feature improves learning, measures capability, or works for a particular population needs research and validation outside this bootstrap.
